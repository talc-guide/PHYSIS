Our Story
The Birth of PHYSIS

It Started with a Simple Question

Is giving a child a desk and chair in class really inclusion?

PHYSIS was born from a question asked by a mother whose child experienced the world differently.

While schools welcomed him, he was often expected to adapt to environments designed for everyone else.

When learning, participation, or adapting became difficult, the responsibility repeatedly fell back on the child and the family.

So she asked:

What if, instead of making the child fit into the environment, we design the environment around the child?

That question became PHYSIS.

Understanding each child

We Begin with the Child, Not the Diagnosis

At PHYSIS, we take time to understand each child before determining the path forward.

We seek to understand their strengths, challenges, interests, communication style, learning preferences, sensory needs, and developmental goals.

The focus is not on defining a child by a diagnosis, but on understanding who they are and what environment will help them thrive.

The PHYSIS Approach

Learning begins with connection.

A child may not initially play football because they understand the rules.

They may simply enjoy kicking a ball.

So we begin with the first step.

01Interest creates connection.
02Connection creates participation.
03Participation creates learning.
04Learning gradually creates mastery.

Rather than insisting on a predetermined method, we seek to discover each child's own pathway into learning.

Mentors at PHYSIS

The Role of the Mentor

Mentors are more than classroom teachers.

They observe, guide, support, and understand the child's everyday journey.

They recognise individual needs, understand communication styles, maintain healthy boundaries, protect dignity, and encourage increasing independence.

They serve as anchors, guides, and champions throughout a child's growth.

Therapy with Purpose

Therapy is not a solution in itself.

It is about scientific approaches and techniques that work only when applied in personalised settings.

Occupational therapy, physiotherapy, speech and communication support, and other professional interventions are incorporated where needed.

Meaningful progress happens only when therapeutic learning extends beyond the therapy room and becomes part of everyday life. What is learned through therapy should help children participate more effectively in classrooms, playgrounds, homes, communities, and relationships.

The goal is not therapy for its own sake. The goal is their participation.

Meaningful Progress

Progress should be visible, meaningful, and relevant to the child's life.

Growth is observed across academic learning, communication, independence, emotional regulation, social participation, physical development, life skills, confidence, responsibility, and overall wellbeing.

Each child is measured against their own journey, not against another child's.

Academic LearningCommunicationIndependenceEmotional RegulationSocial ParticipationPhysical DevelopmentLife SkillsConfidenceResponsibilityOverall Wellbeing

Parents and the PHYSIS team work together to review progress, understand concerns and decide the next set of meaningful goals.

Progress is measured against each child's own journey.

Life at PHYSIS

Like school. Like childhood. Like life.

  • Children learn.
  • They play.
  • They build friendships.
  • They participate in sports.
  • They celebrate.
  • They explore their communities.
  • They prepare for examinations when appropriate.
  • And sometimes, they simply have fun.

Having unique needs does not mean a childhood defined entirely by therapy or intervention. Children need a childhood.

Friendship and Belonging

Belonging cannot be taught through a worksheet.

Children need opportunities to build relationships, communicate, celebrate, negotiate differences, participate, and simply spend time together.

Social development grows through meaningful participation in an active community.

Belonging grows when children have meaningful opportunities to participate together.

Parent Partnership

Parents as Partners

Parents know their child in ways no professional can.

PHYSIS therefore sees parents as partners.

Parents, mentors, and specialists work together to create consistency across school, home, and everyday life while supporting the child's growth and wellbeing.

Together, parents, mentors, and specialists support each child's growth and wellbeing.

Preparing for Life

Preparing for Life

As children grow, the questions change.

Can I communicate what I need?Can I make choices?Can I manage myself?Can I travel safely? Can I build relationships?Can I work?Can I live independently?Can I make decisions safely?

Academic achievement remains important, but so do independence, decision-making, safety, relationships, work readiness, and quality of life.

Education should prepare a young person not only for examinations, but for life itself.

Safety, dignity & wellbeing

Protecting what matters most.

Parents entrust us with what matters most to them.

The physical and emotional safety of every child therefore matters deeply.

Children deserve to be treated with dignity, respect and appropriate boundaries, while being gradually enabled to communicate discomfort, understand personal safety and advocate for themselves.

Protection and independence must grow together.

Our Belief

We do not begin by asking: "What's wrong?"

We ask:

"Who is this child?"

Because when we understand the child, we can adapt the environment, change the method, and unlock new possibilities. PHYSIS believes that inclusion is not about fitting in. It is about designing a world that enables every child to thrive.
